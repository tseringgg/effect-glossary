## Global geometry — read this before any group result

- mean pairwise **cosine** distance between leaf centroids: **0.9798** (median 0.9984)
- fraction of leaf pairs at cosine distance > 0.99 (sharing almost nothing): **74.0%**
- fraction at cosine distance exactly 1.0 (sharing *no* feature at all): **35.9%**
- mean pairwise 2D distance in the layout: 3.54

Most leaf pairs share essentially nothing -- expected for sparse high-dimensional structure, and **not** by itself a problem. What matters for a neighbourhood-preserving layout is whether each leaf has well-defined *near* neighbours, and it does: the median distance to a leaf's 15th-nearest neighbour is 0.848, and only 1.0% of leaves have their 15th-nearest neighbour beyond 0.99. Local structure is real. The flat 1.0 tail means only that *global* distances carry no information -- which is what the rank correlation below measures.

## Embedding faithfulness

- **kNN preservation @10**: 44.4% — of each leaf's 10 nearest neighbours in cosine space, this many survive as 10-nearest in 2D, averaged over all 591 leaves.
- **Spearman rho (cosine distance vs 2D distance, all 174,345 pairs)**: 0.164

A high local score with a low global rho is the normal, documented behaviour of UMAP: it is built to preserve neighbourhoods, not absolute separations. It means *closeness* on this map is meaningful and *distance* is not — 'X is far from Y' cannot be read off it.

## Is it the parameters? (sweep)

kNN preservation @10 across a parameter grid, same seed, same distances:

| n_neighbors | min_dist 0.0 | 0.1 | 0.5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 44.4% | **44.9%** | 43.5% |
| 15 (default) | 44.3% | 44.4% | 39.2% |
| 30 | 40.1% | 39.0% | 31.8% |
| 50 | 38.5% | 37.4% | 28.7% |

The best cell (44.9%) is within noise of the default (44.4%), and every larger neighbourhood is worse. **Tuning does not rescue this layout.** The ceiling comes from compressing genuinely high-dimensional structure into two dimensions, not from the parameter choice.
